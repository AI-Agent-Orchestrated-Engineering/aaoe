# Evidence Standard

## Specification Metadata

**Specification ID:** GV-0005

**Domain:** Governance

**Version:** 1.0

**Status:** Draft

---

# Purpose

This specification defines the role of evidence within the AAOE Body of
Knowledge. Evidence provides the empirical foundation from which
engineering knowledge is derived, validated, refined, and generalized.

Evidence precedes engineering guidance.

---

# Definition of Evidence

Engineering evidence is any observable, reproducible, or verifiable
information that supports an engineering observation.

Evidence exists independently of conclusions. Evidence informs knowledge.
Evidence does not constitute knowledge by itself.

---

# Principles

Engineering evidence shall be:

- observable
- traceable
- reproducible whenever practical
- attributable
- preserved
- reviewable
- open to reinterpretation

Evidence shall not depend upon authority. Evidence shall not become
stronger through repetition alone.

---

# Sources of Evidence

Evidence may originate from:

- production systems
- implementation experience
- architecture reviews
- engineering experiments
- benchmarks
- measurements
- testing
- operational incidents
- postmortems
- migration projects
- modernization efforts
- case studies

Evidence is not limited to successful outcomes. Failures provide valuable
engineering evidence.

---

# Evidence Quality

Evidence may vary in strength. Factors affecting confidence include:

- repeatability
- independent observation
- measurement quality
- engineering context
- documentation quality
- traceability

Higher confidence supports stronger engineering guidance.

---

# Evidence and Discoveries

Discoveries summarize engineering observations supported by evidence.
Evidence supports Discoveries. Discoveries interpret evidence.

---

# Evidence and Specifications

Specifications shall be traceable to one or more supporting Discoveries,
ADRs, Case Studies, or directly documented evidence when appropriate.

Specifications do not replace evidence. Specifications generalize
validated knowledge.

---

# Conflicting Evidence

Conflicting evidence is expected. Conflicting evidence:

- does not invalidate previous observations
- may refine engineering understanding
- may introduce additional context
- may reduce confidence
- may reveal boundary conditions

Knowledge evolves through accumulated evidence.

---

# Evidence Preservation

Evidence should remain available whenever practical. Historical evidence
contributes to future refinement. Evidence should remain traceable even
after Specifications evolve.

---

# Limitations

Evidence alone does not establish engineering guidance. Generalization
requires:

- repeated validation
- engineering interpretation
- contextual understanding
